LPCR introduces an information-theory-based approach for reducing power and energy consumption in serial Network-on-Chip (NoC) links used in manycore computing systems.

The proposed **Low-Power Code Reordering (LPCR)** technique optimizes the assignment of codewords to data symbols to reduce bit transitions during communication. It can be integrated with existing coding methods, including binary, Huffman, and Most Frequent Least Power (MFLP) coding, without requiring major architectural modifications.

### Key Contributions

- **Energy-efficient interconnects:** Reduced switching-related link power consumption by up to **13.2%**, with an average reduction of approximately **8.8%**.
- **Link energy savings:** Achieved up to **9.9% lower energy consumption** when applied to coding methods such as Huffman coding.
- **Low transmission overhead:** Increased transmitted data bits by less than **4.9%** in the evaluated cases, while preserving code length for binary coding.
- **Compatibility:** Applicable to existing serial-link coding schemes without introducing complex encoding and decoding hardware.
- **Low runtime overhead:** Uses small codeword lookup tables to minimize additional computation and storage requirements.
