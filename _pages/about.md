## About Me

I am **Ali Suvizi**, a Ph.D. researcher in Computer Engineering at **The George Washington University (GWU)**, advised by **Prof. Guru Prasadh Venkataramani**.

My research focuses on **computer architecture, hardware–software co-design, high-performance computing, and the design of efficient and reliable computing systems**. I work at the intersection of hardware design, software development, and machine learning, with particular interests in chiplet-based architectures, FPGA/SoC systems, hardware accelerators, edge AI, and energy-efficient computing.

I enjoy developing practical solutions to challenging computing problems, from architectural modeling and simulation to hardware implementation, system integration, and experimental evaluation. My research involves both physical hardware platforms and system-level simulation environments, including Xilinx FPGA/SoCs, gem5, and SST.

At GWU, I have contributed to NSF- and ONR-funded research projects investigating efficient edge computing and resilient hardware architectures. These projects involve collaborations across computer architecture, embedded systems, hardware security, and AI acceleration.

Previously, I earned an M.Sc. in Computer Architecture from **Amirkabir University of Technology**, where I researched parallel computing architectures for accelerating numerical and hydraulic simulations. I also hold a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering (Hardware) from Shahed University.

## Selected Research

### Swift-Healer — Firmware-Reconfigurable Self-Healing Hardware

Developed and evaluated a self-healing architecture deployed on a **Xilinx Zynq-7000 FPGA/SoC** for improving the reliability of autonomous navigation systems under clock and voltage glitch attacks.

The system integrates FPGA acceleration, embedded firmware, machine-learning-based fault prediction, and dynamic hardware recovery. The prototype achieved **0.03 ms/frame accelerator latency**, **0.87 F1-score in fault prediction**, and recovery times ranging from **5 microseconds to 5 milliseconds**.

[Read the paper (ACM GLSVLSI 2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3787109.3815273)

### Auto-Healer — Fault-Tolerant Computing Architectures

Developed a chiplet-inspired FPGA architecture for detecting, diagnosing, and recovering from hardware faults in CNN-based perception workloads.

The research explored redundancy, synchronization, and checkpoint-assisted recovery, reporting repair times of **40 ns for transient faults** and **120 ns for permanent faults**.

[Read the paper (ACM ICS 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3721145.3725755)

### Heterogeneous and AI Hardware Acceleration

My ongoing research investigates efficient computing architectures for machine learning and other data-intensive applications.

This work includes extending the **SST/BYOD system-level simulator** to study hybrid digital–photonic acceleration, RISC-V accelerator interfaces, data movement, numerical precision, and system-level performance.

Additional research explores Processing-in-Memory, FPGA-based inference, and power-efficient LLM fine-tuning.

### Chiplet Architecture and System Optimization

My research also addresses communication, scheduling, and system reliability in chiplet-based computing architectures.

Topics include energy-efficient Network-on-Chip interconnects, real-time scheduling, information freshness, and firmware compatibility across heterogeneous hardware components.

Our chiplet-scheduling research received the **Best Paper Award at IEEE Cloud Summit 2026**.
